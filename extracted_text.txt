Q 9. Can you state the method of dispersion of the data in staƟsƟcs? 
In staƟsƟcs, measures of dispersion, also known as measures of variability or spread, are used to 
describe how data points in a dataset are spread out or dispersed. These measures provide valuable 
insights into the extent to which data values deviate from the central tendency (e.g., the mean) and 
how variable or homogeneous the dataset is. 
Here are some common methods of measuring dispersion: 
 Range: 
The range is the simplest measure of dispersion and is calculated as the diﬀerence between 
the maximum and minimum values in a dataset. It provides an idea of the spread of data but 
is sensiƟve to outliers. 
 Variance: 
Variance quanƟﬁes the average squared diﬀerence between each data point and the mean. 
It is calculated by taking the average of the squared deviaƟons from the mean. 
 Standard DeviaƟon:  
The standard deviaƟon is the square root of the variance. It provides a measure of dispersion 
in the same units as the original data, making it easier to interpret.

Q 14. What is Bessel's correcƟon? 
Bessel's correcƟon is a staƟsƟcal adjustment made to the formula for calculaƟng the sample variance 
and sample standard deviaƟon. It is used to provide a more accurate esƟmate of the populaƟon 
variance and standard deviaƟon when working with a sample from a larger populaƟon. 
The key idea behind Bessel's correcƟon is that when you calculate the variance or standard deviaƟon 
using sample data (rather than data from the enƟre populaƟon), you tend to underesƟmate the true 
populaƟon variance or standard deviaƟon. This underesƟmaƟon occurs because you are basing your 
calculaƟons on a smaller subset of the data. 
Bessel's correcƟon adjusts for this underesƟmaƟon by dividing the sum of squared diﬀerences from 
the mean by (n - 1), where "n" is the sample size. In contrast, when calculaƟng populaƟon variance 
and standard deviaƟon, you divide by "n" (the actual populaƟon size). By using (n - 1) instead of "n" 
in the formula, Bessel's correcƟon increases the calculated variance and standard deviaƟon slightly, 
making them more representaƟve of the populaƟon.

 The formula for calculaƟng the coeﬃcient of variaƟon is as follows: 
Coefϐicient of Variation (CV) = 𝜎𝜇 × 100 
Where:  
CV= Coeﬃcient of variaƟon, σ= standard deviaƟon of the dataset, μ= mean of the dataset. 
 The coeﬃcient of variaƟon is parƟcularly useful when you want to compare the relaƟve

 Example:

Q 17. What is meant by mean imputaƟon for missing data? Why is it 
bad? 
Mean imputaƟon is a method for handling missing data by replacing missing values with the mean 
(average) value of the available data in the same column. 
Disadvantages of Mean ImputaƟon: 
 Bias IntroducƟon:  
Mean imputaƟon can introduce bias into the dataset. 
 Loss of Variability:  
ImpuƟng missing values with the mean reduces the variability of the data because all 
imputed values are the same. 
 Disregards Data PaƩerns:  
Mean imputaƟon does not take into account any underlying paƩerns or relaƟonships in the 
data. It treats all missing values as if they were independent of other variables or condiƟons, 
which may not be the case. 
 Impact on Model Performance:  
In machine learning, mean imputaƟon can negaƟvely impact model performance, especially 
when missing values are related to the target variable or when they carry important 
informaƟon. It can lead to inaccurate predicƟons and reduced model eﬀecƟveness. 
 ImputaƟon of Categorical Data:  
Mean imputaƟon is primarily suitable for numerical data. When dealing with categorical 
data, other imputaƟon methods like mode imputaƟon (replacing missing values with the 
mode, or most common category) are more appropriate.

Q 19. What is the meaning of the ﬁve-number summary in StaƟsƟcs? 
The ﬁve-number summary consists of ﬁve key values that help describe the central tendency, spread, 
and shape of a dataset.  
The ﬁve values in the ﬁve-number summary are: 
a. Minimum (Min): This is the smallest value in the dataset, represenƟng the lowest data point.

b. First QuarƟle (Q1): The ﬁrst quarƟle, also known as the lower quarƟle, is the value below

It gives you an idea of the ceiling or upper boundary of the data. 
 
 
The ﬁve-number summary is oŌen used to create box plots (box-and-whisker plots), which provide a

interquarƟle range (IQR), which is the range between the ﬁrst quarƟle (Q1) and the third quarƟle 
(Q3), while the whiskers extend to the minimum and maximum values, indicaƟng the range of the 
data.

 In other words, it's an observaƟon that is unusually distant from other observaƟons in the

Q 23. What is the impact of outliers in a dataset? 
 
 
 
1. NegaƟve Impacts: 
 Inﬂuence on Measures of Central Tendency: 
A single extreme outlier can pull the mean in its direcƟon, making it unrepresentaƟve of the 
majority of the data. 
 Impact on Dispersion Measures: 
The presence of outliers can inﬂate the measures like the standard devia Ɵon and the 
interquarƟle range (IQR), making them larger than they would be without outliers. 
 Skewing Data DistribuƟons: 
PosiƟve outliers can result in right-skewed distribuƟons, while negaƟve outliers can result in 
leŌ-skewed distribuƟons. This can aﬀect the interpretaƟon of the data. 
 Misleading Summary StaƟsƟcs: 
Outliers can distort the interpretaƟon of summary staƟsƟcs.  
 Impact on Hypothesis TesƟng: 
Outliers can aﬀect the results of hypothesis tests. They can lead to incorrect conclusions, 
such as detecƟng signiﬁcant diﬀerences when none exist or failing to detect real diﬀerences 
when outliers mask them. 
2. PosiƟve Impacts: 
 DetecƟon of Anomalies: 
Outliers can signal the presence of anomalies or rare events in a dataset. IdenƟfying these 
anomalies can be valuable in various ﬁelds, including fraud detecƟon, quality control, and 
outlier detecƟon in scienƟﬁc experiments. 
 Robust Modeling: 
In some cases, outliers can be genuine observaƟons that are important to model. For 
example, in ﬁnancial modeling, extreme stock price movements may contain valuable 
informaƟon for predicƟng market trends.

Q 25. How you can handle outliers in the datasets. 
Handling outliers in datasets is an important step in data preprocessing to ensure that they do not 
unduly inﬂuence the results of your analysis or modeling. The approach you choose for handling 
outliers depends on the nature of the data, the context of the analysis, and your speciﬁc objecƟves. 
Here are several methods for handling outliers: 
 Data TruncaƟon or Removal: 
One common approach is to simply remove outliers from the dataset. This should be done 
cauƟously, especially if the outliers represent valid and important observaƟons. Removing 
outliers is appropriate when they are likely the result of data entry errors or measurement 
errors. 
 Data TransformaƟon: 
Transforming the data can be a useful way to miƟgate the impact of outliers. Common 
transformaƟons include logarithmic, square root, or inverse transformaƟons. These 
transformaƟons tend to compress the range of extreme values. 
 WinsorizaƟon: 
WinsorizaƟon involves capping or limiƟng extreme values by replacing them with a speciﬁed 
percenƟle value. For example, you might replace values above the 95th percenƟle with the 
value at the 95th percenƟle. 
 ImputaƟon: 
For missing values that are not extreme outliers, you can impute them using various 
methods, such as mean imputaƟon, median imputaƟon, or more advanced techniques like 
regression imputaƟon. 
 Robust StaƟsƟcs: 
Using robust staƟsƟcal methods that are less sensiƟve to outliers can be an eﬀecƟve 
approach. For example, replacing the mean with the median and using the interquarƟle 
range (IQR) instead of the standard deviaƟon can make staƟsƟcal analysis more robust. 
 Model-Based Approaches: 
In predicƟve modeling, consider using algorithms that are less sensiƟve to outliers, such as 
robust regression methods or ensemble methods like random forests, which can handle 
outliers beƩer than linear regression. 
 Domain Knowledge: 
Rely on domain knowledge to understand the context of the outliers. SomeƟmes, what 
appears as an outlier may be a valid and important data point. Consult with domain experts 
to determine the appropriateness of handling outliers. 
 ReporƟng and Transparency: 
Regardless of the approach chosen, it's crucial to transparently document how outliers were 
handled in the analysis to ensure the reproducibility and interpretability of your results.

constant p, and binary outcomes).
